# Case Classification and Severity Analysis: Adverse Drug Reaction Case Reports (2026)

The following five case reports, all published in 2026, are classified below according to seriousness and expectedness using standard pharmacovigilance guidelines. Seriousness is assigned when a reaction results in death, is life-threatening, requires or prolongs hospitalisation, causes disability, or is otherwise medically important. Expectedness is assigned based on whether the reaction is consistent with the known safety profile of the suspected drug or is instead a rare, atypical, or previously undocumented presentation.

## Case 1: Fixed Drug Eruption Following Amoxicillin and Paracetamol

**Patient:** A 6-year-old male presenting with fever and sore throat.

**Suspected drugs:** Amoxicillin 500 mg and paracetamol 500 mg.

**Clinical course:** Following repeated exposure to these drugs, the patient developed hyperpigmented erosive skin eruptions with central necrosis affecting the face, lips, and trunk, sparing the genitalia, palms, and soles. A diagnosis of fixed drug eruption was made based on the temporal association with drug intake, lesion morphology, and recurrence on re-exposure.

**Management and outcome:** The suspected drugs were withdrawn, and the patient was admitted to the intensive care unit for monitoring, intravenous steroids, fluid therapy, and topical treatment. Complete recovery occurred after approximately one month.

**Seriousness classification:** Serious, as the reaction required hospital admission and intensive care monitoring.

**Expectedness classification:** Expected, as fixed drug eruption is a recognised and documented cutaneous reaction pattern associated with amoxicillin and paracetamol use.

**Source:** Prasad et al., "Analysis of Seriousness and Preventability of Adverse Drug Reactions in Patients at a Tertiary Care Teaching Hospital: A Case Series," *Cureus*, 2026.

## Case 2: Stevens-Johnson Syndrome / Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis Following Ceftriaxone

**Patient:** A 32-year-old female treated for fever, cough, and expectoration.

**Suspected drug:** Ceftriaxone 1 g, administered intravenously.

**Clinical course:** The patient developed a generalised rash with mucosal involvement of the mouth and lips, atypical target lesions on the trunk, a positive Nikolsky sign, and eye congestion. She was clinically diagnosed with SJS/TEN overlap with multisystem involvement.

**Management and outcome:** The suspected drug was withdrawn, and a multidisciplinary team including dermatology, critical care, ophthalmology, and other specialties managed the patient with corticosteroids, intravenous fluids, mucosal and ocular care, and Ryle's tube feeding. The patient recovered after approximately one month.

**Seriousness classification:** Serious, as the reaction was potentially life-threatening and required hospitalisation and multidisciplinary intensive management.

**Expectedness classification:** Expected, as SJS/TEN is a well-documented, labelled severe cutaneous adverse reaction associated with cephalosporin antibiotics such as ceftriaxone.

**Source:** Prasad et al., "Analysis of Seriousness and Preventability of Adverse Drug Reactions in Patients at a Tertiary Care Teaching Hospital: A Case Series," *Cureus*, 2026.

## Case 3: Acute Renal Failure Following a Diclofenac-Paracetamol Fixed-Dose Combination

**Patient:** A 30-year-old male treated for back pain.

**Suspected drug:** A fixed-dose combination tablet containing diclofenac 50 mg and paracetamol 500 mg.

**Clinical course:** Within two hours of taking the medication, the patient developed limb and generalised body swelling. He was admitted to the intensive care unit and diagnosed with acute kidney injury based on abnormal renal function tests, with a clear temporal association between drug intake and symptom onset.

**Management and outcome:** The suspected combination was withdrawn, and the patient was managed with intravenous fluids, furosemide, sodium bicarbonate, and dialysis. Renal function improved after withdrawal of the suspected drug.

**Seriousness classification:** Serious, as the reaction required intensive care admission and renal replacement therapy.

**Expectedness classification:** Expected, as analgesic-related nephrotoxicity, including with diclofenac and paracetamol combinations, is a recognised risk documented in the literature.

**Source:** Prasad et al., "Analysis of Seriousness and Preventability of Adverse Drug Reactions in Patients at a Tertiary Care Teaching Hospital: A Case Series," *Cureus*, 2026.

## Case 4: Anaphylaxis and Cardiac Arrest Following Initial Intravenous Ceftriaxone

**Patient:** A 61-year-old female presenting with cough and dizziness, with no known antibiotic allergies, being treated for pneumonia.

**Suspected drug:** Ceftriaxone, administered intravenously for the first time.

**Clinical course:** Within thirty seconds of administration, the patient developed severe respiratory distress and cyanosis, followed rapidly by cardiac arrest (asystole). Advanced cardiovascular life support was initiated, and spontaneous circulation returned after two minutes. Transient truncal urticaria appeared and resolved about an hour later.

**Management and outcome:** Ceftriaxone was immediately discontinued, and the patient was stabilised with corticosteroids and supportive care. She was transferred to intensive care and discharged in good health after three days, with no complications at one-month follow-up.

**Seriousness classification:** Serious, as the reaction was immediately life-threatening, involved cardiac arrest, and required resuscitation and intensive care admission.

**Expectedness classification:** Unexpected, since the reaction occurred on the patient's very first exposure to the drug in the absence of any prior allergy history, and anaphylaxis with cardiac arrest on an initial dose is a rare presentation not typical of the drug's routine safety profile.

**Source:** Dai, Liao, and He, "Anaphylaxis and Cardiac Arrest Induced by Initial Intravenous Ceftriaxone: A Case Report," *American Journal of Case Reports*, 2026.

## Case 5: Grade III Anaphylaxis Following Off-Label Rectal Use of Injectable Diazepam

**Patient:** A 14-month-old girl with a complex febrile seizure.

**Suspected drug:** An injectable formulation of diazepam, given rectally as an off-label substitute for an approved rectal gel, at a dose of 2 mg.

**Clinical course:** Within five minutes of administration, the infant developed generalised urticaria, apnoea, cyanosis, and hypotonia, consistent with grade III anaphylaxis, attributed to excipients in the injectable formulation that are not validated for mucosal use.

**Management and outcome:** Emergency treatment included intramuscular epinephrine, corticosteroids, antihistamines, and mechanical ventilation. The patient made a full recovery.

**Seriousness classification:** Serious, as the reaction was life-threatening and required emergency resuscitative treatment, including mechanical ventilation.

**Expectedness classification:** Unexpected, as this reaction resulted from an off-label route of administration and excipient exposure not addressed in the approved product information, and such reactions are documented as exceedingly rare.

**Source:** Nguyen et al., "Severe Anaphylaxis Following Off-Label Rectal Use of Injectable Diazepam in a Febrile Seizure: A Case Report and Clinical Implications," *International Journal of Emergency Medicine*, 2026.
